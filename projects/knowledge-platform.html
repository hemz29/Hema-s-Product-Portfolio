An engineering organisation’s memory is everywhere except where you need it

I’m the Senior Technical Product Lead for an enterprise knowledge engineering platform at a large UK telco. It turns scattered engineering context into one trusted, reusable layer that AI tools across the product lifecycle can build on, so teams ship faster with less rework.

The problem

Ask why a feature was built a certain way and the answer lives in six places: a wiki page, a work-tracker epic, an email approval, a recorded call, a workshop board and somebody’s head. Every new piece of work starts with a hunt through all of them. That hunt is where discovery hand-offs multiply and rework begins.

The model

Four layers, each narrower and more useful than the one before:

1
Knowledge sourcesLeave knowledge where teams already work: wikis, work trackers, documents, email, meeting transcripts, workshop boards, engineering docs and people.
2
A shared context brokerRetrieves the most relevant context for a question and makes it reusable by any downstream tool or agent.
3
Capabilities across the lifecycleStarting with requirements discovery and design assistance. Build, test, deploy and operate come later.
4
OutcomesFaster, more consistent feature delivery, so customers get new capabilities sooner.

My remit

  • Set the direction: own the vision and roadmap, steered by business value.
  • Make value measurable: define success (speed, quality, adoption) and report it in business terms.
  • Bridge business and engineering: connect leadership, architects and delivery teams so the platform solves real problems and gets used.

Strategic priorities

  • Prove value early: start where the pain is, slow requirements discovery and rework, and measure the gains before scaling. The first proof of value is a network-service cancellation journey.
  • Make it trustworthy: answers that are sourced, current and permission-aware.
  • Build once, reuse everywhere: one shared knowledge layer for every AI tool and team.
  • Fit the flow of work: bring knowledge into the tools analysts, product owners, architects and engineers already use.
  • Keep knowledge alive: capture decisions as they happen, so the platform grows more valuable over time.
The grey boxes on the roadmap are a promise, not a claim.

How we’ll know it’s working

Three leading indicators. Faster: requirements validated sooner, with fewer hand-offs. Better: traceable inputs and fewer clarification loops. Reusable: every feature leaves context behind for the next one. Focused pilots measure accuracy, usefulness and how much human correction is needed before we decide to iterate, stop or scale.

This is a developing story. I’ll update it as the pilots report.
