My hypothesis was wrong. The research found a better problem.

I set out to fix a returns annoyance on India’s biggest fashion app. Interviews revealed a quieter cost: shoppers stop trying new brands. The result was a return-rate badge and a skin-tone slider, prototyped end to end.

Starting point

After ten years as a customer, I had a pet frustration: a pricey order arrived without its brand tag, and then the return was rejected because the tag was missing. I mapped the app, the business model, competitors and segments, listed 15 problems, and took this one into research.

What the research actually said

I interviewed five busy professionals and ran a survey. My problem barely registered: no survey respondent had hit it, and the one interviewee who had found the fix easy. What came up again and again was different. When an item doesn’t look like its photos, the return feels like the customer’s fault. “They don’t take return saying product is different from the image.” Mismatches clustered on smaller brands, so shoppers adapted the safe way: they stopped trying new brands.

Shoppers didn’t complain about returns. They quietly stopped taking risks.

The reframe

The problem wasn’t returns. It was confidence before purchase, and it was quietly costing smaller sellers and the breadth of the catalogue.

Choosing what to build

  • Return Transparency Indicator (highest RICE score): every product shows its return rate (low, moderate or high) and the top reasons people sent it back. Support agents see the same data, so a legitimate return isn’t brushed off.
  • Skin-tone slider: adjust the model’s complexion to see how a colour will look on you, not on a studio model.

Measuring it honestly

Primary metric: fewer returns. Secondary: more browsing of smaller brands. Guardrail: the badge will hurt sales of products that are often returned, so that is watched, not hidden. The biggest risk is shoppers not noticing it, so the first step is an A/B test of where it sits.
