Enterprise · Cloud FinOps

Making cloud spend visible to the people who spend it

At a large UK telco, cloud bills were paid centrally and felt by no one. I built the FinOps function that changed that: chargeback across three cloud providers, a champions network in every business unit, and a community of 200+ practitioners.

The problem

When cloud costs land on a central budget, nobody who makes the spending decisions sees the bill. Engineering teams can’t optimise what they can’t see, and finance can’t forecast what nobody owns.

What I built

  • Chargeback across three clouds. I set up and launched the chargeback of AWS, Azure and Google Cloud costs to the teams that use them, so every bill has an owner.
  • A champions programme. I trained champions in each business unit, so FinOps became something units do themselves rather than something done to them.
  • Insight leaders can act on. I set up central reporting on vendor commitments, risk, and people metrics like skill gaps and learning needs, backed by a full risk process to identify, log, monitor and mitigate.
  • A maturity framework. I designed an internal Crawl → Walk → Run assessment, so improvement became continuous and measurable.
  • A front door for cloud. As product owner for the cloud services portal, I gave teams one place to start.
You can’t optimise what you can’t see, and you can’t see what nobody owns.

Capability and community

Tools don’t change behaviour; people do. I grew the FinOps community to more than 200 members and led cloud enablement to upskill colleagues for faster migration. The work was recognised with a transformation award from the CIO organisation.

What it taught me

Making cost visible is a product problem: it needs users, adoption, trust in the numbers and a feedback loop. That is how I approach every platform since.
