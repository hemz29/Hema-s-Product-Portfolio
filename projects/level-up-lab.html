A study app that refuses to teach, and grows a creature instead

Most study apps add more content. Level-Up Lab assumes the textbook is fine and the hard part is showing up, focusing and remembering. A creature grows only when the work is real.

The problem

My daughter’s textbooks are not the problem. Nor are tutors or worksheets. The problem is the forty minutes after she says “I studied”, which can mean anything from real work to staring at an open book. I wanted something that builds the habit: a plan, focused blocks, honest reflection and spaced recall.

The bet

Don’t compete with content. Be the coach and the game layer around the child’s own books, and make the reward depend on evidence of thinking, not time spent.

The reward depends on evidence of thinking, not on time spent.

The decisions

  • The gate. A block only counts once the child writes five takeaways, each with a concrete anchor such as a name, a year, a “because” or a key term. Vague points get gentle coaching from the creature. No pasting.
  • Growth without punishment. Missed days make the creature sleepy, but progress is never lost. Consistency is judged over a forgiving five-of-seven-day window.
  • Mistakes earn rewards. Logging a slip earns play time, so kids learn which errors cost them most.
  • Kids tell their own story. The daily report to the parent is shown to the child word for word before it’s sent.
  • No AI, no ads, no tracking. Admins see usage numbers only, and database rules keep families’ notes private, even from the team.

What shipped

An auto-spread study plan built from the school’s own portion list, 25-minute focus blocks, the takeaway gate, a mistake log, spaced recall, a creature that grows from egg to mythic across three worlds, an art studio, printable exam-style papers, and family accounts that sync and work offline. Every game number lives in one config file, ready to tune after the first trial week.
