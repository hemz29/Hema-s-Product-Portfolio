Teaching India’s grandparents to spot a scam before it spots them

Seniors file fewer than 5% of India’s fraud complaints but lose 18% of the money. Sathi turns scattered government advice into calm, daily lessons that seniors actually finish.

The problem

A “CBI officer” calls with your name, your Aadhaar number and an arrest warrant, and tells you not to hang up or tell your family. Digital-arrest scams like this have taken life savings from retirees across India. The advice that would have helped already exists, but it sits in government PDFs written in formal language, never designed for a 70-year-old reading on a phone.

What I heard

I interviewed five seniors, then validated what I learned with a survey. The surprise wasn’t ignorance. Most respondents actively follow scam news in their circles. The real cost was fear: most now ignore every unknown number, genuine ones included, and some wouldn’t open my survey link at all. “Better not to take a chance.”

The goal changed from warning seniors to giving them back their confidence.

The decisions

  • Government sources only. Every lesson carries a credibility badge and a source link. Nothing is invented.
  • Respect over gamification. No cartoons, no leaderboards. Users are greeted as “Name Ji” by a companion who sounds like a reassuring grandmother. Confetti appears only for a perfect score.
  • Learning before detection. A live fact-checker scored highest on RICE, but it depends on outside services and risks false confidence. I shipped the education layer first.
  • Privacy by default. No account. Sathi never reads messages or calls.

What shipped

Six topics (WhatsApp, SMS links, UPI, phone scams, digital arrest and investment) with voice narration, practice questions with gentle feedback, and a rotating knowledge check built only from completed lessons. A journal records the times you didn’t fall for it, and one tap reaches the national cybercrime portal or the 1930 helpline.

What’s next

A red-flag “scare score” for live situations, short scenario videos, Indian-language support, and a view for adult children who look after their parents.
